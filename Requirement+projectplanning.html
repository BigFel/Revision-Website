Requirements and Planning

Hope this helps you understand the importance of defining clear requirements and planning your project effectively.

Defining the Project

Business Systems Analyst: The person who communicates with clients to identify their needs and writes the technical requirements for the development team.

User Stories: Short, simple descriptions of a feature told from the perspective of the person who will use the software.

Verifiable Requirements: Clear goals that can be measured or tested. A requirement must be written so that a developer can prove it has been successfully completed.

Functional vs. Non-Functional Specifications

Functional Spec sifications: Describe the specific tasks or actions the software must be able to perform.

Examples: Processing a payment, saving a record, or generating a report.

Non-Functional Specifications: Describe the performance and quality of the system.

Examples: System speed, security levels, and ease of use (ergonomics).

System Boundaries

The Boundary: The limit that defines what is part of the system and what is not. It includes everything the developer builds and controls.

The Environment: Everything that exists outside the system boundary, such as users, other software, and hardware.

The Interface: The point where the system and the environment interact and exchange information.
